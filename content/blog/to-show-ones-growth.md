I remembered this site exists after sleeping on it for months. I read one article and wondered if I actually wrote this — and had the sense to publish it online for the world to see. It looked good. It had content. But really? The me from high school could write better essays. I wouldn’t be surprised if the me from elementary could outdo this.

It wasn’t about content. Those articles were pretty adult-sounding — actual critiques and observations of the world around us, not just some short-response question to be answered. The problem was that they read like one. I could barely see the coherence within the article itself. I doubt fresh readers could — technical or not.

I first opened *FixMarker* to use as a reference for something I’m working on now. It was so cringe how I cut myself off by attempting sections. I dared to open the site introduction, *On Building a Space to Think*, and it was much worse. I died at how raw it was. I set this site up as a place to think, but not this level of raw brainstorming. It wasn’t even a brainstorming session; it was me being proud of my work on this site. I decided that if I was proud, I should put more effort into documenting it on this site itself.

I dropped those tech demos. I dropped the versioning system. I hope to drop the old sectioning as well.

Sectioning is a fine structural element. The question is integration. Without it, people can’t follow the thoughts on the screen. With it, the outline itself breaks the train of thought the articles are going for. The old articles were sectioned so loosely that the thought didn’t carry through the article.

I’ve been writing research while the site was dormant. I like to see myself as a writer, so I did some analysis on how they are structured. I suspected they followed a structure outside of “IMRAD” or “5 Chapters.” They did. The introduction is already a complete story. It even comes in two parts, with a position paper being the first one. The second part is an addendum to transition you to the research proper, but even without the succeeding chapters you get a report — an idea of what the research is all about. The methodology continues that story: “This is what we did.” The results and discussion: “This is what we observed.” Why we observed this and not that is another article.

The articles I write must have this flow. Whether or not I follow a structure or a template remains to be seen. So I’ve decided to learn writing from first principles. I lean toward a template because I discovered I’m a bad writer. I couldn’t even understand the “uninspired” stuff I wrote earlier. They were rambles — a regurgitation of topics without showing understanding. They do have merit, but they are not fully-fledged articles.

The old articles made me feel a sense of loss. I had a platform, thoughts, and ideas. The problem is I kept them on my computer. They never got to see the light of day. If there’s anything I learned in college, it’s that art is completed when it has an audience. Whether what I made can be considered art begs to be answered, but the answer lies not here. And that is the source of my insecurity.

Is what I wrote worthy of being read? Am I just being edgy? Is there even merit to publishing this? I still have these thoughts, but we never know if we don’t take that step. I long for a free internet where I don’t need to think about my credibility. I just want to put my thoughts out there, but publishing something means it’s forever attached to my name. That balance is something I still have to think about.

I take these thoughts as a sign of growth. Take this as a reframe and not a redesign. The old me, the new me — if you cringe at the actions you did when you were younger, that’s a sign you’re growing. I don’t like the stance that the moment you’re 18, everything is attached to you forever. But that’s just a fact of life I cannot change for others. At least for me and those who agree, we can choose to believe that is growth. You cringe, you identify what was wrong, and you self-reflect. That’s growth. If you don’t realize this about past actions, then that’s when you get held accountable. You wish to change from your past and do everything in your power to do so. But we cannot erase these records, so that is why I choose to keep them.

There’s a story I keep coming back to. Cornealious Michael “Mike” Anderson III was convicted for his role in a 1999 robbery in Missouri and sentenced to thirteen years. A clerical error kept him out of prison: his bond was never revoked, and the department thought he was already inside. He lived those thirteen years as a free man — married, raised children, ran construction businesses, coached youth football, went to church. In 2013, when his sentence was supposed to end, the error was found. U.S. marshals arrested him. He spent nearly a year in prison while the state decided what to do. In 2014, Judge Terry Lynn Brown counted the 4,794 days Anderson had lived free as time served. The judge said the crime was serious, but Anderson was a different man now. “You’ve been a good father,” he said. “You’ve been a good husband. You’ve been a good taxpaying citizen of the state of Missouri. That leads me to believe that you are a good man and a changed man.” Then he told him to go home to his family.

There’s a chance someone will side with you as you grow. You know who: family, friends, people you rely on. If there’s nobody there, we learn to trust. But there’s a significantly greater population that won’t see your efforts. We shouldn’t let that deter us. For that one who believes in who we are.

The old site wasn’t a mistake. If anything, the process was the mistake. I just went and did it. Greed took over and I wanted to monetize it without understanding what it entails. The attempt itself was brave though foolish. I got to publish what I wanted, and that’s pretty neat.

A redesign would mean throwing out the previous stuff. I did not want to throw out the marks of my growth. The philosophy of this site was to show how I think. Now, not even a year later, this site is where I show my growth.

For now, this article is where the old items stay archived. In the future, they will have a place of their own. This evolution will be linear. No more v1, v2 stuff. No rewrites, just one record with an edited-at date. The rest is discipline.
